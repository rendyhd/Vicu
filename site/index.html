How it works

Organized around when, not where.

No boards and no Gantt charts. A task lands in your Inbox, gets a day, and ends up in the Logbook. Projects and labels are there when you need them.

  1. Inbox

    Where new tasks land, from a hotkey, your voice, a widget or a share.

  2. Today

    Due today, what slipped from before, and today's routines.

  3. Upcoming

    Everything from tomorrow on, grouped by day.

  4. Anytime

    Your open backlog: every task without a day, outside the Inbox.

  5. Logbook

    What you finished, grouped by the day you did it.

Capture

Catch it before it slips away.

Press a hotkey from any app and Quick Entry floats over what you are doing. Type, press Enter, and it is gone. Press another and Quick View shows your list to check off without switching windows.

  • Hold + and say it. Your words appear as you speak, with the date, project and labels picked out. Let go to add it.
  • Lands where you are. A task added in a project goes to that project; one added in Today is due today.
  • Paste a list and every line becomes a task.
  • Works offline. Whatever you capture without a connection is sent once you are back.

Assistant

Talk it through. Get tasks back.

An optional assistant on the AI model you choose. Say what is on your mind and it comes back as tasks with dates, projects, labels and steps, for you to check before anything is added.

  • Smart dictation. Keep talking; each pause turns into task cards. Say "make the first one Friday" and the cards follow.
  • Brain dump. Ramble or type for as long as you like, then Organize sorts it into tasks, in your projects or in new ones where none fit.
  • Ask. "What is overdue in Work?" Short answers, and any change comes as a card you apply or decline, with Undo.
  • Talk. Turn on the microphone in Ask and just talk. The answer is read aloud, and it keeps listening.
  • Break into steps on any task, or turn pasted or shared text, such as an email, into tasks.

Quick capture stays as it was: hold + and let go for one task, without the model.

Organize

As much structure as you want, and no more.

Areas and projects

Top-level areas with projects inside, progress rings and open counts. Drag to nest or reorder, with Undo.

Project categories

Group your top-level projects under headers like Work, Home or Side projects. Drag a project past a header to move it; categories sync to every device.

Sections

Split a project into groups with their own headers and counts.

Custom lists

Your own views by project, label, due window, priority, recent changes, assignee or unread comments.

Labels

Coloured labels with always-readable chips and a page each. Drag a task onto one to apply it.

Routines

Habits next to your tasks, once or several times a day, with history and CSV export. Never overdue.

Project review

Step through your projects on a cadence to keep them current. The status syncs across devices.

Task order

Due date, priority, title, newest or manual, per project. Subprojects follow their parent.

Find anything

Quick find as you type, Search through finished tasks too, and a command palette for every list and action.

Working with tasks

Small touches that keep you moving.

  • One When panel for every date: type "next friday 9am", pick a quick choice, or move through the calendar with the keyboard.
  • A second to change your mind. A finished task stays for five seconds, then collapses. One Undo covers a run of them.
  • Notes, checklists and subtasks, rich text and links, pasted images and attachments.
  • Repeating tasks on any interval; Undo puts the dates back.
  • Reminders at a time, before the due date, or by default on every new dated task.
  • Several at once: select tasks to schedule, move, label, complete or delete them together.
  • Drag and drop to reorder, move to a project or apply a label. On a phone, swipe right to complete and left to schedule.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I need a Vikunja server?

Yes. Vicu is an app for Vikunja, the open-source task server. Run your own, or use Vikunja Cloud. Vicu is built and tested against Vikunja 2.4.0.

How is it different from Vikunja's own web app?

Vikunja's web app is a full project suite with Kanban, Gantt and tables. Vicu is a personal task manager that trades those for speed and a simple flow, with just enough sharing for a household or a small team. They use the same server, so you can use both.

Is it free?

Yes, and open source under the MIT license. The code for the desktop app and the mobile app is on GitHub. If Vicu helps you, you can support it on Ko-fi.

Does Vicu collect anything?

No. There is no Vicu server, account or analytics. Your tasks go only to your Vikunja server, and on iPhone your sign-in stays in the Keychain. Read the privacy policy.

Will my phone and computer agree?

Yes. Both apps follow one written specification for due dates, weeks, custom lists, quick add, routines and task order, and run the same test cases. A task due "friday" is the same Friday everywhere.

What are the hidden tasks I see in other Vikunja apps?

Vikunja has no place for an app's own data, so Vicu keeps custom lists, routines and synced settings in tasks it marks done and hides. You may see them elsewhere, for example "Vicu preferences"; leave them in place.

Do I need an AI model to use Vicu?

No. The assistant is optional and off until you set it up; everything else, voice input included, works without it. If you turn it on, you bring your own provider and key, or a model running on your own machine, and requests go straight from your device to that provider. The key stays on the device and is never synced.

Does voice input work everywhere?

On Android, iPhone, iPad, macOS and Windows. On Linux, holding + opens the composer instead.

Where do I report a bug or share an idea?

Open an issue for the desktop app or the mobile app.
