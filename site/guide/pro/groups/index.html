    Groups, sessions and follow-ups

    Most of a plan is carried out in a few recurring meetings. A group keeps one meeting's actions, issues and decisions together, a session takes you through them, and Follow-ups tells you what needs a move from you in between.

    #In short

    • A group is the people who agree actions together: a leadership team, a project team. A task with the group's label is one of its actions.
    • The group's page shows every action by owner and by where it stands since the last meeting, and a line of plain counts: how many were delivered on time, how many late. Below are the group's issues, blockers, commitments, decisions and open questions.
    • A session is one meeting of the group. It walks through the actions that need a verdict, the issues, what is new, and a read-back. Ending it gives you a recap to copy. Desktop
    • Follow-ups at the top of Today lists what needs a move from you: verdicts due, a check with someone before their action is due, a recap you have not sent, the weekly note.
    • Text you copy (agenda, recap, check message, weekly note) is written for the reader, in the language you chose for the group. Paste it into a mail or a chat.

    Pro

    This page is part of Vicu Pro and builds on Promises and people. Turn on Pro and the part Promises and people in Settings, Pro.

    #Make a group Desktop

    1. Open People and choose New group, or choose New group under Pro in the sidebar.
    2. Give it a name, and choose its label: a new label with that name, or one you already have.
    3. Add the people, in the order they report in the meeting.
    4. Tick the sessions the group holds: weekly, stand-up, or both.
    5. Choose Save.

    The group appears under Pro in the sidebar. A meeting you already made can become a group: open it and choose Make this a group.

    Two more choices are in the same window. Check actions before they are due adds a line to Follow-ups a few working days before an action's date. Language of copied text sets the language of the agenda, the recap and the check messages for this group.

    No longer a group takes the mark off. The label and its tasks stay as they are.

    #Add an action Desktop Phone

    An action is a task with the group's label. On the group's page, New task adds the label for you. Anywhere else, add the label yourself.

    You typeYou get
    $Jan site list friday @leadershipJan's action for the group, due Friday
    $Jan site list friday for $PriyaJan's action, promised to Priya
    $Jan site list friday check on wednesdayThe same, and a line in Follow-ups on Wednesday to look at it
    Draft the on-call rota @leadershipAn action without an owner yet

    @leadership is the label in the default style; in Vikunja style it is *leadership (see Quick add syntax).

    An action has one owner. The owner is the person who promised it; I own this on an action without an owner makes it yours. To give it to someone later, open the task and use Promise.

    Who an action was promised to shows on its row: "From Jan, for Leadership team", "From Jan, for Priya". You give the verdicts on every action you track, whoever it was promised to.

    #Read the group's page Desktop Phone

    The actions are in parts, and in each part by owner:

    • Overdue since the last session: due before the last meeting and still open.
    • Due now: due today or earlier.
    • New since the last session.
    • Open: everything else.
    • Yours: your own actions for the group.
    • Done since the last session.

    Above them is one line such as "Last 4 sessions: 9 of 12 on time, 2 late, 1 open past their date." It counts what happened and is never a score of a person.

    Below the actions are short lists. Each is one line per entry, with no owner and no date: who does what is an action.

    • Issues: things to solve, not to do. They are ranked; move one up or down, and tick it when it is solved. An action that is missed a second time is added here by itself.
    • Blockers: what is in the way and the group has to get moved. Out of the way closes one.
    • Commitments: what the group as a whole said it will reach. Close one when it is met or let go.
    • Decisions, with the day and who decided.
    • Stopped: what the group agreed to stop doing, so it stays said. Close one when it no longer needs saying.
    • Open questions, to tick when they are answered.

    A group's 90-day plans are listed on its page too.

    #Run a session Desktop

    On the group's page choose Start weekly or Start stand-up, then Start session. Nothing is timed.

    1. Actions. The actions that need a verdict come first, by owner. Click the person's mark on a row, or press 1 to 5, to say what happened: Delivered, Late, Not yet, New date or Dropped. A stand-up shows only what is due this week. Mark who is not here.
    2. Issues (weekly only). Add issues, rank them, tick the ones you solved, or make an action of one. Blockers are here too: add what is in the way, close what is not any more.
    3. New actions and decisions. Type new actions as people commit to them; add what was decided, what the group committed to, what it stops doing, and questions nobody could answer.
    4. Read back. The decisions and new actions of this session, to read aloud before you close, so everyone leaves with the same list. Tick Read back to the room when you did. You can give the session a score from 1 to 10 for yourself; it is never copied.

    End session writes the session and opens its recap. Leave without saving drops the session; verdicts and tasks you already added stay.

    Tip

    Before the meeting, Copy weekly agenda on the group's page gives you the actions due, the other open actions, the open issues and the open blockers as text to send round.

    #Send the recap Desktop

    The recap holds this session only: decisions, commitments, what was stopped, new actions, what was delivered, what changed, issues solved, open issues, blockers, open questions, and who was absent.

    1. Check the text. Change Language if this one should go out in another language.
    2. Choose Copy for formatted text (mail, Teams, Word) or Copy as Markdown.
    3. Paste it where the group reads it.

    Copying marks the actions in the recap as sent and the decisions as shared; Undo on the message takes that back. Choose Later to do it another time: the recap waits in Follow-ups for two weeks.

    #Work through Follow-ups Desktop Phone

    Follow-ups is at the top of Today while something needs you. In order:

    • Verdicts due. A promise whose date has come: give the verdict on the row.
    • Checks. A few working days before someone's actions are due, one line per person. Copy message gives you a short note ("Hi Jan, checking one thing: ...") to paste into a chat, and marks the actions as checked.
    • Look at. A date you set with check on or Check on in the task's Promise. Checked clears it.
    • Recap not copied, for a session you ended without sending its recap. Desktop
    • The weekly note is due, on the day you chose. Desktop

    Dismiss for today hides a line until tomorrow, on this device. Follow-ups never sends a notification.

    How many working days before the date a check appears, which days are working days, and the note's day are in Settings, Pro, Follow-ups. A group can turn its checks off, and a single action can: open it, choose Promise and tick No check before due.

    #Write the weekly note Desktop

    The weekly note is a short status for the people above you: per group, how many actions were delivered on time and late, each date that slipped and why, what was decided, the open issues and blockers, and what is due next week.

    1. Open the command palette and choose Weekly note, or choose Write it in Follow-ups.
    2. Read it, change the language if needed, and choose Copy or Copy as Markdown.

    The next note starts the day after this one was copied. It is a draft for you to edit before you send it: add the two sentences only you can write.

    #Details

    • Where it is stored. A group is a meeting in Pro's hidden record on your server, with the label's id. Sessions, issues and decisions are in the same record. Actions are ordinary tasks with the label, so a device or app without Pro still sees them.
    • Older history moves out of the way. Records of a group older than 90 days move to Pro's archive; the counts and the pages read the last sessions.
    • Languages. The text for other people comes from Vicu's translations. A sentence that is not translated yet is written in English. See translating Vicu.
    • On the phone. Phone The phone has Follow-ups in the drawer and the group's page (from People, or a group label's menu), with verdicts. Groups are made on the desktop or the web, and sessions, the recap and the weekly note are there too; the phone says so on those lines. A check message shared from the phone is written in the language the phone shows Vicu in.
    • Turning Promises and people off hides groups, sessions and Follow-ups. The labels, the tasks and the record stay.
